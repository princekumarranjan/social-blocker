Features

Small tool. One job.

Wapas does one thing: it notices when a quick look turned into a long scroll, and it interrupts you.

Counts reels and shorts

Works on Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Snapchat and Facebook in your desktop browser.

Time limits per app

Set daily minutes for each app. When the minutes are gone, you get a nudge.

The limit shrinks

Skip a meme and the next one comes sooner: 10 reels, then 8, then 6, never below 4.

Shows your task

Type “Maths homework” once. The meme reminds you what you were supposed to be doing.

Private by design

Counts stay on your device. Wapas reads only the page address, never your messages or posts.

Free

No ads, no tracking, no payment. Just a meme with good timing.

Interactive demo

Press “Scroll a reel” until the meme lands. Dismiss it and watch your next limit get smaller.

Guides

Understand the scroll.

Short, practical reads on why we keep scrolling and how to take back your time.

Why do we keep scrolling?

Feeds never end, so there is no natural moment to stop. Each swipe might bring something great or something boring, and that uncertainty is what keeps people swiping. Reels also autoplay, so the default action is to keep watching. Stopping needs a decision; continuing needs none. Wapas adds the missing stopping point.

How to build healthier screen habits

Start with one rule, not ten. Pick a limit you can actually keep, for example one 10-reel session after study. Put your phone out of reach while working. Replace the habit, don’t only remove it: a short walk or a drink of water fills the same “break” slot. Review once a week and adjust.

How to manage Instagram usage

Turn off notifications you don’t need and log out on your study device. Set a daily minute limit for the app, in Wapas or in your phone’s own settings. Notice what you open it for: boredom, a break, or a habit. Decide ahead of time when you will check it, so it stops being the thing you do between everything.

Digital wellbeing guides

Screen time is not bad by itself; what matters is whether you chose it. Watch for signs: losing track of time, feeling worse after scrolling, or skipping sleep. Basic care helps: no screens in the last hour before bed, regular breaks, and time with people offline. If scrolling feels out of your control, talk to someone you trust.

Productivity and focus guides

Work in blocks of 25 to 50 minutes, then take a real break away from feeds. Write the one task you are doing and keep it visible. Wapas shows it on the meme for this reason. Start the task for just five minutes; starting is usually the hardest part. Keep your phone in another room if you can.

Original research and useful insights

Some numbers, from simple arithmetic you can check: 45 minutes a day on one app is about 11 full days a year. Ten reels of 30 seconds each is five minutes, which feels like nothing but repeats many times a day. A limit that shrinks 10, 8, 6, 4 stops you after 28 reels in total.
